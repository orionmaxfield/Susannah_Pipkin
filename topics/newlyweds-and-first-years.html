Newlyweds and the First Years of Marriage

“Starting your sexual relationship well”

Nobody warns you how much there is to figure out. Going from “don’t” to “do” in a single evening is a big transition, and many couples spend their first year quietly wondering whether what they’re experiencing is normal.

It usually is. Awkwardness, mismatched expectations, pain, performance anxiety, and the slow process of learning each other’s bodies are all part of starting out. We want to help you begin with grace, patience, and a lot less pressure.

What we believe

  • Great sex is learned, not automatic. Give yourselves time. The first year is for exploring, not performing.
  • Pain is not something to push through. Painful sex is common and treatable. Please talk to a doctor or pelvic floor therapist.
  • Keep talking. The habits you build now, like talking openly and offering gentle feedback, will carry you for decades.
  • Laugh together. Humor and kindness do more for a new sexual relationship than any technique.

Questions people ask

Straight answers

Is it normal for the wedding night to be awkward?

Completely. Most couples describe their wedding night as some mix of sweet, clumsy, and tiring. Lower the bar, focus on being together, and let the rest come with time.

Sex hurts. Is that normal?

Common, yes. Normal to just endure, no. Pain during sex can usually be treated. See a doctor, and ask about pelvic floor physical therapy. Please don’t wait years, as many couples do.

We don’t know what we’re doing. Where do we start?

With curiosity and patience. Learn each other’s bodies slowly, talk about what feels good, and remember there is no test to pass. Episode 1 is a good place to start for the mindset.

How often should newlyweds have sex?

There is no right number. What matters is whether both of you feel wanted, safe, and free to say yes or no. Frequency tends to sort itself out when those pieces are in place.

These answers are educational, not a diagnosis. If this topic is causing ongoing pain in your marriage, please reach out to a licensed therapist.
