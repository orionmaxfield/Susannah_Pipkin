Sexual Shame and Faith

“Feeling guilty about sex, even in marriage”

Many faithful couples spend years hearing “don’t,” and then expect everything to flip to “yes” on their wedding night. It usually doesn’t. The guilt, the hesitation, and the sense that your body is somehow suspect don’t disappear just because you knelt across an altar.

Here’s the good news: shame is not doctrine. The gospel teaches that sexual intimacy in marriage is sacred, good, and ordained of God. Shame is what gets layered on top by culture, well-meaning lessons, past experiences, and sometimes trauma. We help you tell the difference.

What we believe

  • God is not a shaming parent. He designed intimacy for marriage and delights in His children enjoying what He gave them.
  • Sexuality and theology don’t have to be at odds. You can be fully faithful and fully passionate.
  • Not everything needs a confession. Things like sex toys or exploring what feels good with your spouse are not sins to bring to your bishop. We say so out loud because so many couples quietly wonder.
  • Healing is possible. Past trauma, resentment, and years of pressure can be worked through, often with the help of a therapist.

Questions people ask

Straight answers

Why do I feel guilty having sex with my husband?

Because years of messaging don’t turn off overnight. Guilt after marriage is extremely common and is almost always about conditioning, not sin. Naming where the message came from is the first step to letting it go.

Are sex toys okay for Latter-day Saints?

Nothing in Church doctrine forbids married couples from using tools that help them enjoy each other. This is a private matter between you, your spouse, and the Lord. It is not something you need to confess.

What if my past makes intimacy hard?

Trauma, a difficult sexual history, or repentance that still feels unfinished can all show up in the bedroom. Please consider working with a licensed therapist. You deserve more than white-knuckling through it.

Can I be faithful and still love sex?

Yes. That is the entire premise of this podcast.

These answers are educational, not a diagnosis. If this topic is causing ongoing pain in your marriage, please reach out to a licensed therapist.
