Pornography and Shame in LDS Marriage

With Dr. Annie Bryner and Dr. Susannah Pipkin

About this episode

Pornography and shame can be difficult to discuss in a religious marriage. In this episode, Dr. Annie Bryner and Dr. Susannah Pipkin, certified sex therapists and active Latter-day Saints, explore emotional coping, the difference between guilt and shame, and how couples can talk about pornography with more openness.

Many Latter-day Saints have only heard this topic discussed in a context of fear and shame. This conversation takes a calmer, clinical look at what is actually going on, and what helps people change.

Key takeaways

  • It is rarely just about sex. Pornography use often becomes an escape from loneliness, boredom, and difficult emotions. Understanding what it is soothing is part of changing it.
  • Guilt vs. shame. Guilt can be a healthy signal that we acted against our values. Shame tells us we are broken, and it tends to keep people stuck.
  • “Out of control sexual behavior,” not “addiction.” The hosts explain why they choose this language, and how seeing it as a behavior, instead of an identity, opens a path to change.
  • The late-night “shame hours.” Resolve is weakest late at night, when people are tired and alone. Knowing that helps couples and individuals plan for it.
  • Openness instead of fear. Couples can talk about pornography in their marriage without panic or accusation, and the hosts share how to start.

Memorable moments

It’s not about sex… It’s often about escape or soothing.
Guilt is not our enemy… It’s the shame we don’t want to feel.
I don’t use addiction language when it comes to sexual imagery use.
The most important thing to call ourselves is a child of God.

Questions to talk about together

Try one of these with your spouse this week. Pick a calm moment, and listen more than you talk.

  1. How was pornography talked about when each of us was growing up? What did that teach us to feel?
  2. When I feel lonely, bored, or stressed, what do I usually reach for?
  3. What is the difference between guilt and shame for me? Where do I feel each one?
  4. What would help us talk about this topic with openness instead of fear?
